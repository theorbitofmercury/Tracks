      <td style="width: 877px; vertical-align: top;";font size="3">
        In the Summer of 1994, Veronica, Paul and Hannah and I (Mike Webb)
        completed our first long distance walk the famous Wainwright Coast to
        Coast path from St Bees Head to Robin Hood Bay in England. This had
        been a very enjoyable walk and gave us the inspiration to plan further
        walks. The concept of a coast to coast walk was very appealing in that
        the start and end are clearly and naturally defined. So the already
        existing and indeed the first official coast to coast path called the
        Southern Uplands Way (SUW) in Scotland became the ideal challenge.
        After a couple of rest years (walking was not forgotten in these either
        but not long distance) 1997 was to be the year for the SUW challenge.
        By this time Paul was now 18 and being more independent and having a
        part time job decided not to join in but instead was available for
        transport to the start and also back from the end if required. This was
        very useful as the start and end are both remote from main
        communication lines.

        was also a particularly good year for this walk as both Hannah and Paul
        were doing their respective GCSE and "A" level exams. This meant that
        when the exams finished in the second half of June they were not
        required at school/college any longer the walk could take place early
        in the Summer. This was ideal as it was known that both the weather and
        midges get worse during July/August.<br />
        <br />
    </td></tr>
    <tr>
      <td colspan="4" style="width: 323px;">We were
        reasonably well equipped having done the Coast to Coast walk 3 years
        earlier but we had recently bought a Colman's gas stove (separate
        burner type) which was excellent and also in 1996 Veronica and I had
        bought Gortex waterproof coats and better waterproof trousers. The
        tents we already had together with Karimor rucksacks were still in
        excellent condition. I had been given the SUW official guide by Roger
        Smith (HMSO) as a Christmas present from Mum and Dad in 1996 and this
        included 1:50,000 strip maps of the whole way. We had sent for the
        accommodation guide which was published by the Dumfries and
        Galloway/Scottish Boarders Councils. This is an indispensable list of
        the scantly available accommodation.<br />
        <br />
      </td>

    <tr>
      <td colspan="2" style="width: 323px;">On
        Saturday 28 June we travelled to the start at Portpatrick, Galloway,
        Scotland. This was over 350 miles from Cambridge so was an all day
        affair leaving at 1000 and arriving at 1700. The journey was good with
        Paul sharing the driving with myself. The weather was fair with some
        rain on the way but dry when we arrived. We decided to book in B
        &amp; B after a brief look around at a couple of the three
        campsites offered, mainly because we wanted to guarantee a dry start
        for the first day and the weather looked uncertain. The B &amp; B
        was excellent (&pound;15 each) with the landlady offering ginger
        cake and biscuits to settle us in. She recommended the Harbour House
        Inn for dinner and also recommended a walk for half a mile south on the
        coast to see a ruined castle (Dunskey). We enjoyed the walk to the
        castle which was well worth the visit and then had an excellent dinner
        at the Harbour House Inn which was very reasonable.<br />

        It had been hard but we all enjoyed it being proud of our achievement.
        Its not a walk we would like to do again in a hurry but that's because
        there are so many others to do. We obviously compared it with the
        Wainwright Coast to Coast which we probably enjoyed more. This was
        because on the SUW we met so few people and this made for long days.
        Often on the Coast to Coast, walking with others helped to make you
        forget the sore feet! The bothies were a highlight and in particular
        the White Laggan bothy which is an exquisite location made all the
        better for us by having it to ourselves. The log fires will always be
        remembered!<br />

        lead to a slow deterioration of our bodies. A couple of rest days would
        have been useful. Also the lack of decent food for some of the days did
